Welcome

Welcome to CMSC 198.1

For CMSC 198.1, our focus is simple: to develop a solid proposal for your Special Problem (SP). You will identify a meaningful problem, determine how computing can help address it, examine what already exists, and define a scope that is both relevant and feasible.

There is no implementation yet. This semester is about establishing what you intend to do, why it matters, and how you plan to assess it. By the end of CMSC 198.1, you should have a clear and well-developed proposal that will serve as your blueprint for CMSC 198.2, where you will develop, test, and defend your system.

Direction

Main Goal

As my advisees, we conduct research that brings together innovation, relevance, and impact. This means exploring new methodologies, addressing novel or persistent problems, developing creative solutions, and contributing meaningfully to both society and scholarship.

In other words, your SP should not simply be about building a system. It should address a problem worth solving and demonstrate why your proposed solution matters.

Deliverables

Requirements

  • 01Attend scheduled private checkpoint meetings (typically every other week)
  • 02Submit the SP proposal in LaTeX format
  • 03Defend your SP proposal at the end of the semester, with a PPT presentation

Cadence

Private Meetings

Regular private meetings are designed to maintain open and effective communication between us and to ensure that you are making consistent progress. These meetings are largely informal, so you do not need to present something perfect every time.

For each meeting, prepare a short PPT covering your recent progress, key updates, and any questions or concerns you would like to discuss. Feedback and suggestions will be provided in real time, and we can clarify issues and work through concerns together.

After each meeting, you will refine your proposal or project based on our discussion. This process continues throughout the semester as a continuous learning and feedback loop, allowing you to improve your work with each iteration until the end of the term.

Reference

CMSC 198.1: SP Manuscript Structure

The outline below mirrors the structure your proposal should follow, chapter by chapter.

01

Introduction

This chapter establishes the foundation of the study by presenting the problem context, rationale, intended objectives, significance, and boundaries of the study.

ABackground of the Study

Provides the context and justification for the study or project. This section should explain:

  • the real-world problem, unmet need, or computational or practical challenge being addressed
  • the broader domain or environment in which the problem exists
  • existing systems, practices, or current approaches
  • limitations, inefficiencies, or gaps in current approaches
  • why the problem is important
  • why a computing solution or approach is appropriate or needed
What context gave rise to this study, and why does it matter?

BStatement of the Problem

Clearly defines the specific problem(s) the study seeks to address. This section should:

  • translate the broader background into focused problem statements
  • identify computational, technical, practical, informational, or user-centered issues, as applicable
  • clarify what is currently lacking or inefficient
  • logically establish the need for the proposed computing solution

This may be presented as a general problem statement followed by specific problem statements.

What exactly is the problem this study intends to solve?

CObjectives of the Study

States what the study intends to accomplish. This section should include a General Objective and Specific Objectives. Depending on the nature of the SP, objectives may involve:

  • analyzing the problem, existing approaches, or stakeholder needs
  • identifying technical or functional requirements
  • designing and developing the proposed computing solution, method, model, or framework
  • evaluating its usability, accuracy, effectiveness, efficiency, acceptance, or other relevant measures
  • comparing the proposed approach with existing methods or baselines, where applicable

Objectives should be specific and measurable, with each objective corresponding to something that can eventually be demonstrated, tested, or evaluated in CMSC 198.2.

What does the study aim to achieve?

DSignificance of the Study

Explains why the study matters, who may benefit from it, and how. Depending on the study, potential beneficiaries may include:

  • end users
  • organizations or institutions
  • administrators or decision-makers
  • practitioners or operational personnel
  • researchers and future developers
  • broader communities
  • the computing field or body of knowledge

Explain the specific contribution or value of the study to each relevant beneficiary rather than simply listing stakeholders.

Who benefits from this study, and in what ways?

EScope and Limitations

Defines the boundaries of the study by clearly establishing what is covered and what is not. This section should clarify:

  • the specific problem or aspects of the problem covered by the study
  • the intended users, context, or application domain, where applicable
  • the features, functionalities, methods, or components included
  • the data, technologies, platforms, or environment covered, where relevant
  • the extent of implementation, testing, and evaluation
  • relevant boundaries that affect how the results should be interpreted

Avoid listing minor constraints or unnecessary details that do not materially affect the study. Focus only on boundaries and limitations that are important for understanding the scope of the work and, eventually, interpreting its findings.

What does the study cover, and what important boundaries should the reader understand?
02

Review of Related Literature

Provides a critical review of relevant studies, technologies, methods, frameworks, systems, and theories related to the proposed SP.

This chapter should:

  • organize related literature by theme or topic rather than simply discussing sources one by one
  • synthesize the key ideas and findings from existing work
  • compare and contrast relevant approaches, methods, technologies, or systems
  • identify limitations, gaps, or opportunities in existing work
  • establish how the proposed study relates to, builds upon, improves, or differs from existing work
  • provide sufficient basis for the proposed approach and design decisions

Use reputable and relevant sources, prioritizing recent literature, preferably published within the last five years. Older sources may be included when they are foundational or particularly relevant to the study.

What does existing work tell us about the problem, and how does the proposed study build upon or differ from what has already been done?
03

Methodology

This chapter describes how the SP will be designed, developed, implemented, and evaluated. The methodology should provide enough detail for the reader to understand how the objectives of the study will be achieved.

Writing guidance: Present the methodology, design rationale, and technical approach at an appropriate academic level of abstraction. Focus on the methods and design decisions necessary to understand, reproduce, and evaluate the study. Avoid turning this chapter into software engineering documentation, developer manuals, or code-level implementation walkthroughs.
How will the study be conducted, and how will the proposed solution be designed, developed, and evaluated to achieve the study objectives?

AResearch Design

Describe the overall approach that will guide the study. Depending on the nature of the SP, this may include:

  • developmental or design-oriented research
  • experimental research
  • quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods
  • evaluation-centered approaches
  • other appropriate computing research approaches

Explain why the selected approach is appropriate for the objectives of the study.

BRequirements and Data Preparation

Describe the data, requirements, or other inputs needed for the proposed solution, as applicable.

For data-driven studies, this may include:

  • dataset source and method of collection or generation
  • dataset characteristics, size, features, classes, or structure
  • preprocessing, cleaning, transformation, or feature extraction
  • data partitioning or sampling procedures
  • relevant ethical, privacy, or data protection considerations

For system-oriented studies, this may instead include:

  • functional and non-functional requirements
  • user or stakeholder requirements
  • system inputs and outputs
  • relevant operational or technical requirements

Include only the elements relevant to the proposed SP.

CDevelopment Methodology

Describe how the proposed computing solution will be designed and developed.

C.1 Conceptual Overview

Provide a high-level explanation of the proposed solution and how its major components work together. Appropriate diagrams, models, or flowcharts may be included.

C.2 Technical and Theoretical Foundations

Present the algorithms, models, computational principles, architectures, frameworks, or other foundations on which the proposed solution is based. Mathematical formulations or formal definitions should be included where relevant.

C.3 Design and Implementation

Describe how the proposed solution will be constructed. Depending on the project, this may include:

  • system or software architecture
  • algorithms and pseudocode
  • model architecture and training procedures
  • database or data design
  • interface or component design
  • parameter selection and optimization
  • integration of external services or components
  • implementation procedures

The level of detail should be sufficient to explain how the proposed solution will be developed and implemented in CMSC 198.2.

C.4 Tools and Technologies

Identify the major tools and technologies that will be used in developing the proposed solution, such as:

  • programming languages
  • frameworks and libraries
  • databases
  • APIs and cloud platforms
  • AI/ML models or services
  • development tools

Briefly justify important technology choices when they materially affect the design or implementation of the study.

DTesting and Evaluation Methodology

Describe how the proposed solution will be tested and evaluated. Depending on the objectives of the study, this may include:

  • technical performance metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, RMSE, latency, or response time
  • usability measures, such as SUS, task success rate, or learnability
  • user evaluation or perception measures
  • comparison with baseline methods, models, or existing solutions
  • functional and reliability testing
  • performance, stress, or load testing
  • experimental procedures and testing conditions
  • appropriate statistical analysis, where applicable
The testing and evaluation methodology should directly correspond to the objectives of the study. Each objective that requires validation should have an appropriate method, measure, or criterion for determining whether it has been achieved.
04

Project Timeline

Presents the planned schedule of activities for CMSC 198.2, from development and implementation through testing, evaluation, and final defense.

  • Provide a week-by-week or month-by-month schedule, as appropriate.
  • A Gantt chart is preferred for presenting the timeline.
  • Ensure that activities and milestones are realistic and aligned with the CMSC 198.2 semester or project period.
  • The timeline should reflect the major activities described in the methodology.
How will the proposed work be carried out within the CMSC 198.2 project period?
B1

Required Back Matter 1: Declaration of AI Use

Provides a transparent account of how generative AI or other AI tools were used in preparing the proposal. This section should briefly identify:

  • the AI tools used, if any
  • the specific purposes for which they were used
  • the parts or stages of the work where AI assistance was involved
  • how AI-generated outputs were reviewed, verified, or modified by the student

Students remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, integrity, and quality of the submitted work, regardless of AI use.

The use of AI tools is not, by itself, a basis for penalty. What matters is transparent and responsible use, with students remaining accountable for the work they submit. If no AI tools were used, this should also be explicitly stated.
B2

Required Back Matter 2: References

Lists all sources cited in the manuscript.

  • Use APA format consistently.
  • Ensure that all in-text citations have corresponding entries in the reference list, and vice versa.
  • Verify the accuracy and completeness of all reference information.
  • Include only sources actually cited in the manuscript.
